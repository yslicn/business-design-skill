A structural transition under profit-pool migration — the opportunity is not chasing growth, but capturing the existing base.

Core Judgment The profit pool is migrating from "vehicle manufacturing + conventional powertrains" into "batteries + intelligence + aftermarket services"; the crisis facing joint ventures is structural, not cyclical. 01Executive Summary

Seven Design Conclusions (I) · 01–04

The first four steps of the VDBD path — market scan, customer selection, value proposition, profit model.

01
Market Scan
China's passenger-vehicle market has shifted from an "incremental dividend" to "redistribution of the stock": the profit pool is migrating from vehicle manufacturing + conventional powertrains toward batteries + intelligence + aftermarket services. The JV crisis is structural — the opportunity is not chasing growth but capturing its own installed base.
02
Customer Selection
Target the existing-customer trade-up segment — market attractiveness is high (81.2% of trade-ins flow to NEVs, upgrades to 200k+ price bands) and the company's right to win is relatively strongest (30 million installed customers + 1,000 dealers + quality and service reputation) — one of the very few target groups "pushed in front of the company by both policy and product forces."
03
Value Proposition
Offer "German-quality certainty × NEV powertrain satisfaction × trade-in convenience" — establishing a third identity between "local NEV brands" and "ICE-era JVs," so that loyal customers' first NEV is still this brand.
04
Profit Model / Value Capture
Rebuild from "selling-car margin" to a "complete trade-in solution (customer solution) + service profit pool (installed base)": NEV gross margin as the gateway, ICE aftermarket cash flow as transition resources, and finance/used-car/extended-warranty as the long-term profit layers; software subscription is only a premium differentiator, not a revenue source.

01Executive Summary

Seven Design Conclusions (II) · 05–07

The final three steps of the VDBD path — activity scope, strategic control, risk management.

05
Activity Scope
Do the complete loop of capturing the installed base — NEV manufacturing (mainly PHEV/EREV) + channel restructuring + the service chain; do not fight the "chasing growth" battles — in-house batteries, full-stack in-house autonomy, sub-¥100k price wars, hard competition above ¥300k, or near-term exports.
06
Strategic Control
Protect profit through four non-replicable control points: 30-million customer data, the 1,000-dealer channel and service reputation, brand equity, and ICE cash flow — a moat that buys "time to convert installed-base assets into growth-point assets"; autonomy and platform capabilities are locked in through partnerships, not built in-house.
07
Risk Management
The biggest strategic risk is not "transitioning too slowly" but "subsidizing NEVs with ICE profit inertia without reaching the scale to form a positive loop" — both shareholders must commit to a 3–5 year profitability tolerance and hold the 2026–2027 window under three timing pressures.
In one sentence Rebuild growth and brand momentum by "capturing the installed base" — not chasing growth, but re-engaging 30 million existing customers: NEVs as the gateway, aftermarket services as the value-capture layer, the ICE base as cash flow and time, four control points to protect profit, and the 2026–2027 window held under three timing pressures.

01Assumptions & Validation

Key Assumptions & Validation Plan

Ten assumptions determine whether the design holds — each with an impact chain and a validation method.

A01Existing-customer trust is convertible
Impact: precondition of the "capture the base" path — if trust is broken, the 30-million base cannot convert.
Validation: NPS segmentation research + 2026–2027 trade-up pilot conversion rates, benchmarked against the 81.2% industry trade-in flow.
A02PHEV/EREV is the primary capture form
Impact: decides the product route; if BEV becomes mainstream while PHEV supply lags, the window is missed again.
Validation: track PHEV/EREV penetration and JV PHEV sales; verify form preference in trade-up research.
A03New-platform 40% cost-down is achievable
Impact: the life-or-death line for unit economics — the only systematic cost lever besides batteries.
Validation: BOM teardown of new-platform models vs. the incumbent platform; single-car margin disclosure after the first NEV launch.
A04Autonomy supply arrives "smart-ready"
Impact: decides whether the 2026–2027 product window holds; autonomy is a core purchase decision (L2+ penetration 67.6%).
Validation: city NOA availability/satisfaction tests; supplier delivery milestone tracking.
A05ICE aftermarket cash flow can fund the transition
Impact: the resource source for "funding the transition with ICE profits"; cash-flow gap if the base shrinks faster than NEV ramp-up.
Validation: quarterly monitoring of ICE fleet, aftermarket revenue and NEV service revenue share.
A062026–2027 purchase-tax window can be captured
Impact: window-period shipments decide the ramp slope; after rollback, product strength — not policy — is tested.
Validation: monthly tracking of window-period sales and post-rollback demand.
A07Installed-base data is reachable and convertible
Impact: sets the ceiling of the capture business; without precise reach, the base advantage cannot be monetized.
Validation: 30-million customer data-modeling pilot to establish reach/intent/conversion baselines.
A08¥5,000–7,000 per-unit net margin is sustainable
Impact: bounds the profitability estimate; if price wars drive unit margin to zero, the model must pivot faster to services.
Validation: quarterly per-unit net margin and peer benchmarking; actual margin after new-platform cost-down lands.
A09No export/overseas profit in the near term
Impact: prevents overseas premiums from entering the profit model; estimates are China-only.
Validation: no overseas revenue assumptions until the JV's export strategy is explicit.
A10Shareholders commit to a 3–5 year profitability tolerance
Impact: sets the resource ceiling and pace; prioritizing near-term profit risks missing the window.
Validation: written governance confirmation of the NEV budget and a 3–5 year breakeven timetable.
02Market Scan · Five Views

Market Scan · Five Views (I) — Industry / Market / Competition

Three views on "profit-pool migration" and "redistribution of the stock".

View 1Industry trends: volume up, margins thin, profit pool migrating
2025 auto production/sales: 34.53M / 34.40M units; passenger cars first topped 30M, but the industry profit margin fell to a historic low of 4.1%, further to 3.4% in Jan–May 2026. The battery profit pool is ~¥70–120bn (15–26% of total auto industry profit); OEMs' most valuable conventional-powertrain pool has been taken away; the downstream ICE aftermarket pool is restructuring (NEV per-vehicle service value down 30–34%). NEV retail penetration was ~54.1% in 2025 and reached 62.8% by June 2026; JV-brand NEV penetration is only 11.9% versus 81.8% for local brands — an order-of-magnitude gap.
E01E02E21E27E04E07E08
View 2Market & customers: trade-ins drive the market, trade-up is migration
Growth now comes from trade-ins and additional purchases, not first purchases: the trade-in share exceeded 60% in 2025; 81.2% of ICE owners pick an NEV as their trade-up first choice, and trade-ups come with consumption upgrades (after trade-up, 74.1% buy above ¥200k, with ¥200–300k the mainstream band). Purchase factors rank as safety/range/price; only 13.25% will pay a premium for intelligence — "high attention, low willingness to pay, low trust." The two JVs hold a combined 20M+ ICE fleet, and the automaker itself has built 30M+ installed customers, now entering the concentrated replacement window.

View 3Competition: local brands overtake, JVs defend
Local-brand share rose from 38.4% (2020) to nearly 70% (2025), while JVs shrank from ~60% to ~30%; in the ¥100–200k core band, local share is 65.2% and BYD holds 19.2%. Profit paths have clearly diverged — vertical integration (BYD), exports (Chery), premium (Seres), extreme cost-down (Leapmotor); among JVs there are no "growth" players left, only "defenders." City-NOA third-party suppliers are highly concentrated (Momenta 60.2%, Huawei HI 20.6%), and software subscription is hard to monetize in China.

Market Scan · Five Views (II) — Capability / Window

Own capability — "strong installed-base control points, missing growth control points" — and an opportunity window "bounded by 2026–2027, with the white space in reach."

View 4Own capability: strong installed-base control points, missing growth control points
Sales fell from a 2019 peak of 200.2k units to 102.4k in 2025, and 2026H1 was −31.2% YoY; the three ICE cash-cow models (mainstream sedan >270k, business sedan ~234k, main SUV >203k) remain cash pillars, and the smart-ICE line sold ~122k units, +220%, validating "ICE + intelligence." 02Market Scan · Policy

Market Scan · Policy Windows

Six policy variables stack into a determinate 2026–2027 window — and into timing pressure after the rollback.

Policy 1 · E47
Purchase-tax rollback
2024–2025 exempt (≤¥30k/vehicle), 2026–2027 halved (≤¥15k), expires end-2027; total 2024–2027 relief ≈ ¥520bn. 2026–2027 is the last determinate policy window.
Policy 2 · E48
Dual-credit hard constraints
NEV credit ratio rises to 48% in 2026 and 58% in 2027; of 87 onshore automakers, 19 were negative in 2025 — 17 of them JVs/importers; PHEV credits fall to 0.5, steadily eroding the P&L with compliance costs.
Policy 3 · E49
Trade-in subsidies
2025: over 11.5M trade-ins, lifting new-car sales >¥1.6tn; 2026 rules: scrappage→NEV 12% up to ¥20k, trade-in→NEV 8% up to ¥15k — policy aligns with the replacement wave, effectively moving ICE-base customers for automakers.
Policy 4 · E50
L3 admission
MIIT issued the first L3 permits in December 2025 with two models piloted in Beijing/Chongqing — the commercialization pace decides how fast high-level autonomy value is realized.
Policy 5 · E51
China VII emissions
Expected to be finalized 2026–2027, piloted 2028, national from 2029–2030; sharply raises ICE engine and aftertreatment costs — a second squeeze on ICE-base profit that accelerates weaker-player exit.
Policy 6 · E52
NEV going rural
Cumulative ~15M units sold over five years, underwriting the low-tier market and matching the downward shift of NEV customers.

The automaker is deeply tied to a segment where profit is leaking — it must migrate toward NEV manufacturing and aftermarket services in parallel.

Current segment
Mid-stream · vehicle manufacturing (ICE-led)
Largest revenue but lowest-margin segment — industry net margin 4.1%, squeezed by both price wars and cost structure. JV share fell from ~60% to ~30%, and 2026H1 sales were −31.2%. Continuing to farm this old pool means farming a shrinking pool.
Migration · dual move
NEV manufacturing (capture gateway) + aftermarket services (value-capture layer)
Use NEV manufacturing as the gateway to capture the 30-million base, and the aftermarket service layer to monetize long-term profit — the two fulcrums of the "capture the base" system.

It must migrate toward NEV manufacturing and aftermarket services simultaneously.

03Customer Selection · Detail

Segment Assessment Detail (I) · Segments 1–3

Why NEV manufacturing is the gateway, ICE is defense, and distribution must expand.

① Mid-stream · NEV manufacturing (PHEV/EREV-led + BEV supplement, ¥150–250k)
Enter-expand · Gateway
Rationale: vehicle segment revenue ¥11.18tn but net margin only 4.1%, per-unit ceiling ¥5,000–7,000 — large volume, thin margin (3); NEV penetration 54%→62.8% with triple policy tailwinds, value keeps flowing in (4); local brands hold 65.2% of the ¥100–200k band and the JV starts near zero (2); platforms and manufacturing exist but batteries/autonomy are external, and the 40% cost-down is unproven (3); 30M installed customers + 1,000 dealers + ICE cash flow are highly synergistic — products are the only vehicle for capture (5); concentrated external delivery but timing risk (3).
Verdict: enter-expand. Not the highest score (3.35 < 3.50), but the gateway of the whole "capture the base" value chain — winning here unlocks the downstream service profit pool, so it is the recommended primary segment.
② Mid-stream · ICE manufacturing (current base)
Defend
Rationale: the three ICE models remain cash pillars (3); ICE output fell >9% cumulatively over three years with purchase-tax rollback and China VII double-squeezing, and the powertrain pool has migrated out (1); JV share shrank to 30% (2); this is the automaker's strongest segment — brand/manufacturing/channel full-stack (5); ICE cash flow is the resource source for "funding the transition" (4); long-term contraction is irreversible, and the longer it is delayed the fewer resources remain (2).
Verdict: defend — not a growth engine, but the transition-period cash flow and customer-relationship base; use the "ICE + intelligence" line to slow attrition, positioning ICE as "cash flow and time" rather than a long-term business.
③ Downstream · distribution & retail (channel restructuring)
Expand
Rationale: channel profit has shifted from new-car margin to aftermarket (80.8%) and finance/insurance (36.2%) (3); the wholesale-price-differential model is structurally exhausted — 81.9% price inversion, −25.5% new-car margin — while the "order-based + low inventory + service income" NEV channel is profitable (3); the national 4S network is contracting but the NEV agency model is proven (3); a 1,000-dealer channel exists yet the NEV storefront network shrank from 154 to 58, exposing touchpoint weakness (4); the channel is the first touchpoint for trade-in capture and highly synergistic with the 30-million base (5); dealer satisfaction hit a low of 60.8 and restructuring needs investment and interest redistribution (3).
Verdict: expand — restructure toward an "OEM-controlled pricing, dealer-as-service" hybrid: transparent pricing protects new-car margin, dealer profit shifts from wholesale margin to service commissions; restore NEV outlet density in the ¥150–250k band.
03Customer Selection · Detail

Segment Assessment Detail (II) · Segments 4–6

Aftermarket is the value-capture layer; batteries are avoided, intelligence is partnered.

④ Downstream · aftermarket services (service + used cars + finance/insurance)
Expand · Value-capture layer
Rationale: multiple trillion-level downstream pools stack — service ¥1.0–1.2tn, used cars ~¥1.29tn, auto finance ~¥3tn, NEV insurance premiums ¥190bn (4); high-margin ICE service items disappear with electrification, but battery inspection/used-car appraisal/extended warranty grow — total stable, structure reshuffling (3); NEV service/used-car competition is relatively fragmented and the traditional 4S aftermarket remains the dealer profit pillar (3); a service network and 30M installed customers exist, but battery inspection and used-car appraisal need building or partnering (3); highly synergistic with the ICE base and the trade-in chain (5); new capabilities take time and used-car/finance risk must be managed (3).
Verdict: expand — build "service + finance + used car + extended warranty" into a transition-period cash flow and long-term profit pool; it depends on first winning the NEV segment to close the customer loop, so it is a synergy layer of the primary segment rather than a standalone entry.
⑤ Upstream · batteries
Avoid in-house entry
Rationale: battery pool ¥70–120bn, 15–26% of industry profit, leader net margin ~17% (5); installations +40.4% but prices fell 40% cumulatively with profit re-concentrating to leaders (4); CR2 ~65% highly concentrated, CATL/BYD-dominant (1); the automaker has no battery capacity/technology and vertical integration is impossible (1); supplier-only relationship, no synergy (2); huge investment, entry too late, clashing with the JV structure and profit-first constraints (1).
Verdict: avoid — the highest market attractiveness (1.95) but the lowest enterprise fit (0.60); the textbook case of "highest margin ≠ most worth entering"; manage cost through long-term supplier lock-in and second-source strategy, no in-house build.
⑥ Upstream · intelligence (autonomy domain control + smart cockpit)
Partner, not build
Rationale: autonomy domain control only ¥47.9bn, smart cockpit ¥156.4bn — still small (2); domain control CAGR 109.9%, autonomy a core purchase factor (L2+ penetration 67.6%) (5); city-NOA suppliers concentrated (Momenta 60.2%/Huawei 20.6%) and BYD pressed hardware to ¥3,000 (2); the automaker relies on external supply with no in-house stack (2); autonomy is key to product competitiveness but can only be accessed via partnership (3); in-house entry is unrealistic, external supply from Q3 2026 is the only practical path (2).
Verdict: partner — achieve "smart-ready" through external suppliers (autonomy provider + smart-cockpit and chip partners), making autonomy a standard feature rather than a paid item (software subscription is not dependable in China); no full-stack in-house build.
03Customer Selection · Decision

Recommended Segment · The System Combination

"NEV manufacturing as the gateway, aftermarket services as the value-capture layer."

Recommendation · enter / expand Primary segment: NEV manufacturing (expand, 3.35), with aftermarket services and distribution & retail (both 3.50) as the synergistic capture layers. Conclusion: NEV manufacturing (PHEV/EREV-led) as the strategic gateway, channel and services as capture layers, the ICE base as defense, and in-house batteries/autonomy as avoid/partner.
Reason 1 · Gateway logic
The two highest-scoring downstream segments (aftermarket/distribution) essentially depend on "whether customers buy the automaker's NEVs" — only by first winning NEV manufacturing (the carrier for capturing 30M customers) can the downstream service profit pool be captured; it is the gateway of the whole system.
Reason 2 · Strongest right to win
Market attractiveness is middling (1.70) but enterprise fit is the company's strongest (1.65, business synergy full marks) — 30M installed customers, a 1,000-dealer channel and quality/service reputation are unreplicable starting assets.
Reason 3 · Counter-example
The selection deliberately avoids the "highest-margin" battery segment (attractiveness 1.95 but fit 0.60, 2.55 — the lowest total) — exactly the VDBD judgment that "highest margin ≠ most worth entering."

05Profit Model · Candidates

Profit Model · Candidate Patterns

Five candidates — from gateway to capture layer.

C01 · Customer Solutions (Complete Trade-in)
Primary pattern
Bundle "old-car disposal + new car (PHEV/EREV) + subsidy filing + financing/insurance + extended warranty" into one complete trade-in solution for A1, carrying the entire replacement journey in a single low-friction offer; profit comes from new-car margin + financing/insurance commissions + extended warranty, and bundling protects the transaction price (avoiding one-point price wars). Fit: the only pattern built on non-replicable control points — 30M installed-customer data (E61), the 1,000-dealer channel (E89) and the policy trade-in dividend (E49); trade-in programs (11.5M units / ¥1.6tn, E49) have made replacement a policy-driven real need — evidence is strong.
C02 · Aftermarket / Installed Base — service pool
With 30M installed customers + 1,000 dealers as the installed base, capture service profit from "aftermarket + extended warranty + used cars + financing/insurance"; ICE transition cash flow (¥1.0–1.2tn service, 80.8% of dealer gross margin) funds the NEV build, shifting long-term to battery inspection/used-car/extended warranty. Fit: a traditional strength and the only pool still JV-dominated; the cash-flow profile matches the "fund the transition with ICE profits" rhythm.
C03 · Time Profit — 2026–2027 first-mover
In the overlap of the purchase-tax window (expires end-2027, E47) and external tech delivery (autonomy from Q3 2026, new-platform 40% cost-down, new EEA, E90), be first to offer A1 "smart-ready + PHEV/EREV" certainty and build the "JV existing-base" mindshare before competitors. Fit: matches the 2026–2027 launch cadence; but the window is externally given and expiring — a supporting, not primary, pattern.
C04 · Brand — rebuilt pricing power
Rebuild the "German quality × smart-ready" perception so ¥150–250k NEVs earn psychological pricing power above pure value rivals, and underwrite service and trade-in trust premiums. Fit: the underlay of the differentiated proposition and an amplifier for retention and resale; but rebuilding takes time and product proof — a supporting pattern, not a standalone revenue source.
C05 · Profit Multiplier — platform/channel reuse
Reuse the new platform/EEA (E90), external autonomy supply (E90), the 1,000-dealer channel (E89) and 30-million data (E61) across "PHEV/EREV/BEV" product lines and "new car/service/finance" profit points — the same customer contributes new-car, financing, extended-warranty and used-car profit within one lifecycle. Fit: interlocks naturally with C01 + C02; but it generates profit only through the quality of other patterns — a layering pattern.

Architecture conclusion Primary pattern: customer solutions (C01 — complete trade-in), bundling "old car + new car + subsidy + financing + extended warranty" to capture A1 and protect pricing while locking in follow-on profit; supporting patterns: aftermarket/installed base (C02, transition cash flow and long-term profit), time profit (C03, 2026–2027 first-mover), brand (C04, pricing power). Why C01 as primary rather than C02?
The strategic task is "capture the base" — if only the aftermarket pattern monetizes the 30 million existing customers, they will still drift to local NEV brands (81.2% prefer an NEV for trade-up), and the service pool will dry up as the base shrinks. Only by using the complete trade-in to keep the first NEV inside the brand (new-car margin + financing + extended warranty) does the aftermarket gain a durable installed base. Hence C01 is the gateway, C02 the capture layer. Software subscription is rejected on direct evidence (12.8% paid penetration, 31% frequent use); relative market share / experience curve are rejected because the ¥100–200k scale contest cannot be won against BYD.

Unit Economics

Revenue, cost and margin formation per NEV — labeled as directional.

Unit of analysis
Per NEV (PHEV/EREV-led, ¥150–200k core band, China basis, RMB).
Revenue formula
Transaction price (¥150–200k, near the ¥170k anchor) − promotion/financing subsidy − portion of trade-in subsidy passed through = net revenue; price supported by the new-platform cost-down (target 40% vs. the incumbent platform), staying above the 8% gross-margin JV red line.
Variable cost

Contribution-margin logic
Industry per-unit net-margin ceiling is ¥5,000–7,000 (~1/3–2/5 of Toyota); sustain positive contribution through vehicle gross margin (new-platform cost-down + PHEV/EREV reusing the ICE powertrain) and lift combined per-unit economics with service profit (aftermarket/finance/warranty) — bare-vehicle profitability depends on scale and cost-down.
Working capital & cash conversion
Capacity utilization ~38% strains fixed-cost absorption; order-based production + low-inventory NEV channels cut inventory and working capital; ICE aftermarket cash flow serves as the transition working-capital reservoir.
Sensitivity
Sales (ramp pace), transaction price (every −1% erodes full margin), battery cost (35–50% of BOM), capacity utilization (38%→55%+ fixed-cost absorption), trade-in conversion.

08Risk Management

Risk Management · Six Dimensions

The biggest risk is not "moving too slowly" but "subsidizing NEVs without forming a positive loop."

Risk 1 · Policy
Risk: purchase-tax halving expires end-2027 and the price gap closes; dual-credit negative costs rise (48%/58% ratios) and China VII squeezes the ICE base a second time, compressing transition cash flow. Mitigation: define 2026–2027 as a "pull-forward + capture" window, maximize window shipments with 7 launches; use PHEV/EREV to hedge dual-credit/China VII; prepare post-rollback pricing plans.
Risk 2 · Competition & industry
Risk: price wars keep eroding per-unit margin (terminal discounts 27.77%, JV gross margin below 8%); responding with price would trigger a "volume-for-price" trap. Mitigation: do not fight with price — protect the transaction price with the complete trade-in + service profit, use the platform cost-down to convert room into share rather than loss, and cede the lowest local-price segments.
Risk 3 · Technology supply
Risk: if autonomy, the 40% cost-down or the new EEA slip, the 2026–2027 product window is missed — the single point of failure of an externally dependent transition. Mitigation: put supply milestones under JV governance, set delay-triggered contingency plans (backup suppliers/re-sequencing); maintain the smart-ICE floor until supply arrives.
Risk 4 · Organization & JV
Risk: tension between the group's "profit-first" stance (2027 ROS 3–5%) and the transition's speed; if ramp losses are not tolerated, investment is cut; long JV decision chains respond slowly. Mitigation: secure written governance commitment to "3–5 year profitability tolerance + NEV budget + breakeven timetable"; prove self-funding via ICE aftermarket cash flow to reduce dependence on group capital.
Risk 5 · Customer churn
Risk: the 30-million base sits on a migration path where 81.2% prefer an NEV for trade-up; if products don't capture it, the "ammunition" flows entirely to local brands (Chinese brands now ~70% of the consideration set); attrition is already happening. Mitigation: make "capture the base" the top strategic priority with data assetization + complete trade-in; raise retention through service reputation and retention management.
Risk 6 · Financial
Risk: ICE cash flow shrinking (E08) faster than NEV ramp (−31.2% in 2026H1) creates a cash-flow gap; 38% utilization burdens fixed costs; per-unit NEV cost has no public data, making estimates uncertain. Mitigation: order-based production + low inventory to control working capital; use aftermarket cash flow as the transition reservoir; put per-unit cost and utilization into validation tests and data gaps.
